History

Overview

Prior to the modern digital age, all records were created and maintained physically in secure sites. Over the past few decades, the scope of the internet has grown rapidly alongside its widespread accessibility to the point that approximately 400 million terabytes of data are generated every day. This exponential growth of generated data is impossible to maintain physically, hence the trend towards digital record keeping. The ability to instantly reference a digital database of records invites a variety of data applications. This access to wholesale data paired with increasingly intelligent AI models creates the notion of an individual’s data as a commodity. These developments require an ethical framework to maintain data privacy, security, and transparency.

Case Study: Johnson Controls Ransomware Attack (2023)

A recent case in which ethical standards were not met is the Johnson Controls ransomware attack which took place in September of 2023. The attack was conducted by the Ragnar Locker ransomware group which took advantage of vulnerabilities in regional offices in Asia, to encrypt and steal 27 terabytes of data. Johnson Control, which primarily creates custom industrial control systems, held proprietary designs, trade secrets, and client details. However, this data included building floor plans and physical security details for various federal agencies and defense contractors. The group was later discovered to have had unauthorized access to Johnson Control’s internal network for eight months prior to being noticed and reported. This case highlights how even a major international corporation with endless resources can be susceptible to an attack caused by a simple act of negligence.

Ethical Implications

Transparency: Corporations have an ethical obligation to disclose a breach of data security to their users promptly. Failure to do so further endangers the public and delays efforts to resolve the issue.

Corporate Responsibility: In the modern landscape, many corporations use the same cloud computing platforms, hence a breach in one company may expose a vulnerability in others in that sector. Corporations have an obligation to disclose experienced vulnerabilities to prevent a similar breach.
